# Research statement

Adrien Joly, London School of Economics. November 2026.

At STICERD, working with Professor Xavier Jaravel, part of my research studies how artificial intelligence is implemented within firms and how it changes the way people work, individually and in teams.

My own research looks upstream, at what this reorganisation of work implies for young people and for policy. First, how we inform and orient students: I am building a compass of university courses and apprenticeships (work in progress) that links each path to its risk of displacement from AI and from Chinese import competition. Second, short-term AI-driven unemployment, especially in entry-level roles. Two further themes follow: how to bring frontier AI to the many at a reasonable cost, and how AI can change the way universities and governments work together.

**1. Entry-level hiring and the changing role of juniors.** When AI produces the first draft, the everyday tasks on which newcomers used to learn a job shrink. I study what firms now expect of junior employees, how daily work is split between newer and more experienced staff, how newcomers still build expertise and judgment, and how the wider economy should adapt to this changing role.

**2. What students should learn.** My work asks which human skills matter most for succeeding in the AI transition. Over recent decades, the labour market has rewarded social skills more and more (Deming, 2017). The open question is which specific capabilities, such as teamwork, decision-making and judgment, grow in value when AI handles routine analytical work, and what schools and universities should teach.

**3. Guiding students into careers.** Current careers guidance still rests on a picture of jobs from before AI. I study how we can improve the way young people are oriented towards university studies and early careers by using concrete evidence on which occupations and specific tasks AI is exposing and transforming, and I examine which public policy proposals follow from that evidence.

**4. Access.** I study how to democratise the adoption of frontier AI: who is currently left out of the best tools, and how we can bring frontier models to firms, schools and public bodies in a way that is both efficient in computing power and cost-effective for public and private budgets.

**5. Universities and government.** Standard economic analysis can now be automated with AI, and is easy to set up. Today, academic and policy deadlines differ, effort is duplicated on both sides, and there is too little consultation. Bringing frontier AI tools into academia, alongside better coordination of priorities, could close this timing gap and let universities such as LSE offer governments timely advice on pressing issues.
